To assistive technology a mockup is a picture, and it is described like one.

## The canvas is an image with a name

The canvas has `role="img"` and an accessible name. By default a mockup names
itself after its model - "3D mockup of a Galaxy S phone" - and a bare
`MockupCanvas` says "3D mockup". That is correct but rarely useful; say what
the picture shows, the way you would write an image's `alt` text:

```tsx
<GalaxyMockup label="Our budgeting app's monthly overview on a Galaxy phone">
  <MonthlyOverview />
</GalaxyMockup>
```

## Screen content is hidden by default

Whatever is on the screen is decorative, like the pixels in a product photo:
by default (`screenAccessibility="hidden"`) every screen layer is marked
`aria-hidden="true"` and `inert`. Screen readers skip it, the Tab key never
lands in it, and a demo's headings and links do not end up in your page's
outline. Describe what matters in `label` or in the page text around the
mockup.

When the screen carries meaningful text that appears nowhere else on the page,
expose it with `screenAccessibility="visible"`:

```tsx
<IPadMockup label="The pricing page on an iPad" screenAccessibility="visible">
  <PricingTable />
</IPadMockup>
```

Keep links and buttons off a screen you expose: they would be announced, but a
pointer can never reach them - [screens are display-only](/docs/screen-content#why-screens-are-display-only).

## Keyboard

With `controls` on (the default), the canvas is focusable, and the keys do
what a drag does:

| Key | Action |
| --- | --- |
| `←` `→` | Turn the model |
| `↑` `↓` | Tilt the model |
| `+` `-` | Zoom in and out (when `zoom` is on) |
| `Home` | Reset the view |

Make sure your page's styles leave a visible focus indicator on it. The zoom
and full-screen overlay buttons are real `<button>` elements with accessible
names ("Zoom out", "Zoom in", "View full screen").

A mockup that is pure decoration - nothing to turn, nothing to read - can drop
out of the tab order with `controls={false}`.

## Reduced motion

`autoRotate` and `float` stop for visitors whose system asks for
`prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, and the dot-matrix `LEDText` marquees hold on
their first frame. Gestures still work - a drag, a pinch or a key press is the
visitor's own request. Nothing is required of you - unless you drive them
from your own clock with `time`, which hands the decision to you; see
[Reduced motion](/docs/api/mockup-canvas#reduced-motion) for the hook the
library uses, if your page has motion of its own to gate.

## Touch

A mockup never traps page scrolling on a touch screen: vertical swipes over it
scroll the page, horizontal drags turn the model. Turning `zoom` on hands
two-finger gestures to the canvas. See
[Touch behavior](/docs/screen-content#touch-behavior).

## On this site

The home page carousel advances on its own, and a Pause button under it stops
and restarts that. Any arrow, swatch or drag stops it too, it waits while it is
off screen or the tab is hidden, and under reduced motion it does not
auto-advance at all unless you press Play. Only the changes you make are
announced to screen readers, not every automatic step.
