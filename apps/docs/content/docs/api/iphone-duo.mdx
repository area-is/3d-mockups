`<IPhoneDuo>` and `<IPhoneDuoMockup>` render Apple's iPhone Duo - the first
foldable iPhone, on the same passport shape as the wide Galaxy Z Fold 8. One
device, two form factors: **open** (default) fills your content onto the
landscape 7.6″ inner display, which has no camera hole to design around - a
faint crease runs down its center - and **closed** (`openAngle={false}`) folds
to the 5.4″ cover screen with the corner camera, and the two-lens plateau and
the Apple badge on the back.

To render this model, pass `variant="duo"`.

```tsx
import { MockupCanvas, IPhoneDuo, IPhoneDuoMockup } from 'react-3d-mockups'

// The one-liner: its own canvas, with lighting, a ground shadow, drag-to-orbit
// controls and a camera already framed on the object.
<IPhoneDuoMockup variant="duo" float style={{ height: 520 }}>
  <YourApp />
</IPhoneDuoMockup>

// The same model with no stage of its own. Inside a canvas you arrange
// yourself it can be posed, animated, lit and joined by anything else.
<MockupCanvas>
  <IPhoneDuo variant="duo" rotation={[0, 0.4, 0]}>
    <YourApp />
  </IPhoneDuo>
</MockupCanvas>
```

## Props

Beyond the [shared screen props](/docs/screen-content) and, on the mockup, the
[shared mockup props](/docs/api):

| Prop | Type | Default | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `variant` | `'duo'` | `'duo'` | Which iPhone Duo to render - the 2026 original is the only model today. |
| `openAngle` | `boolean \| number` | `true` | How far the book is open. `true` is the unfolded tablet (content on the landscape inner display, with a center crease), `false` the folded bar (content on the cover display, camera pill and badge showing). A number is degrees (0 = shut, 180 = flat) and renders the real book pose: the panels pivot around the hinge while its bare titanium cover stays tangent to both back shells, wrapping the fold at every angle, and your content bends across the crease on two composited planes. The pose is continuous: every angle renders its own pose, and only the extremes hand over to the dedicated folded (~0°) and flat (180°) poses, which draw the crease as one seamless screen. |
| `orientation` | `'portrait' \| 'landscape'` | `'portrait'` | Rotates the device a quarter turn and swaps the virtual display with upright content. The open Duo is natively landscape, so `landscape` stands it upright into 626×890, like turning the real tablet onto its short edge. |
| `statusBar` | `boolean \| StatusBarOption` | `false` | Draw the system status bar across the top of the screen. `true` gives iOS's defaults - on the cover screen the clock and the meters sit in the ears either side of the camera hole; the inner display has no hole, so it takes iOS's plain tablet strip; an object sets the clock, the meters and the ink (`{{ time: '14:05', battery: 0.42, batteryPercent: true, color: '#1c1c1e' }}`). See [Status bar](/docs/screen-content#status-bar). |
| `color` | `string` | `'#3a3d42'` | Back glass. Takes a colorway id from `IPHONE_DUO_COLORWAYS[variant]` (see [Colorways](#colorways)), which brings the mirror-polished titanium rail with it, or any CSS color - the metal is then derived from it. |
| `resolution` | `number` | per state | CSS pixel width of the active display; height follows the panel aspect. Defaults to Apple's point grids for whichever screen is showing - 890×626 open, 466×678 closed - see the [device table](/docs/devices). |
| `coverScreenUntil` | `number` | `30` | The hinge angle, in degrees, up to which the cover display stays lit as the device opens; above it your content moves to the inner display, so one of the two is lit at every angle, as on the hardware. `30` is where Android's reference foldable swaps; `90` keeps the cover on through the half-open tent pose, `0` swaps the moment the hinge moves. |

## The two states

The active display is whichever screen is lit, so your content maps onto the
cover display from shut until the hinge passes `coverScreenUntil` (30° by
default) and the inner display after it. Switch with a single prop:

<MockupExplorer
  component="IPhoneDuoMockup"
  variant="duo"
  props={{ openAngle: false, autoRotate: true, float: true, color: 'starwhite', surfaceBackground: '#000' }}
/>

## Any angle in between

`openAngle` takes a number of degrees to pose the book at any openness - the
half-open standing pose with the hinge cover wrapping the fold and your content
bending across the crease:

<MockupExplorer
  component="IPhoneDuoMockup"
  variant="duo"
  props={{ openAngle: 110, color: 'nightsky', rotation: [0, -0.35, 0] }}
/>

Between open and closed, once the inner display is lit (past
`coverScreenUntil`), each half of it is its own screen, so your screen content
is mounted once per half: effects, state and video run twice. Fully open,
shut, or while the cover display is lit, it is mounted once. Each half lays
the content out at the whole display's size and shows its own part of it, so
`useSurface()` reports the whole display in both halves - the same numbers as
open flat.

## Regions

One region: the screen (bare children). `Screen` follows whichever display is
lit - the cover display from shut until the hinge passes `coverScreenUntil`,
the inner display after it. Use the explicit `<IPhoneDuoMockup.Screen>` slot
when you want per-surface overrides - `surfaceBackground`, `resolution`,
`surfaceStyle`. The same slot exists on the bare `<IPhoneDuo>`.

## Colorways

Pass a colorway id from `IPHONE_DUO_COLORWAYS[variant]` for the retail finish;
it brings the mirror-polished titanium rail with it. Any CSS color works for a
custom finish, and the metal is then derived from it. An id wins over a CSS
color of the same name - pass hex if you mean the CSS one.

| Model | Colorway ids (body color) |
| --- | --- |
| iPhone Duo (`duo`) | `nightsky` (`#2a323e`), `starwhite` (`#f1f0ec`) |

## This model

### iPhone Duo

Apple's published body: folded 117.8 × 84.1 × 11.3 mm, unfolded 117.8 × 164.6
× 5.2 mm, on the same vertical hinge as the Galaxy Z Fold. The cover is a 5.4″
1398×2034 panel with a 6 mm camera hole in its top-right corner; the inner
display a 7.6″ 2670×1878 panel with its FaceTime camera *under* the glass, so
nothing interrupts it. The detail geometry follows Apple's own product-bezel
drawings: the two 48 MP cameras sit side by side on a horizontal 56 × 21 mm
plateau across the top of the camera half, with the mic grille and flash at
its inner end and the badge centred below; the free-edge corners are 12.4 mm
round while the hinge ends are nearly square. Touch ID lives in the side
button, alone on the free rail; the two volume keys ride the camera half's top
edge; there is no Camera Control. The frame is mirror-polished grade 5
titanium and the hinge cover is bare, micro-blasted titanium with no wordmark.
