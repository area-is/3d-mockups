Every device is procedurally generated at runtime, built from published
specifications and product photography: no GLB files, no hosting, no pop-in. Variants within a family keep true
relative sizes because they share one world scale. Non-device mockups (books,
brochures, billboards, a cargo van, …) live on the
[Objects and print surfaces](/docs/objects) page.

**Resolution basis.** The Galaxy S26 (and the foldables' FHD+ panels) are
their panels at one-third scale. The QHD+ S26 Ultra uses One UI's default FHD+
render at 450 dpi. The Fold 8's 1248×1972 cover is its panel at 1/2.6 (the
density bucket nearest its true 163 dp/in), its 2448×1848 inner panel divides
by the Fold 7 inner's own 2.4, and the Fold 8 Ultra's sharper inner panel
keeps the Fold 7's 910×820 grid outright (2504/910 = 2256/820 = 2.75). Galaxy Tabs use their xhdpi dp grid (panel at half scale,
an estimate since Samsung does not publish logical grids). iPhones, iPads, the
Watches, the MacBooks, and the Studio Display use their exact point grids
(2x or 3x) - the iPhone Duo's inner 2670×1878 panel at 3x is 890×626, given
open as the unrotated landscape width, as the Fold 8's is.

## Orientation

Pass `orientation="landscape"` on any phone or tablet to lay the device on its
side. The virtual viewport swaps to the landscape resolution and your content
renders upright, exactly like rotating the real device.

Two devices run the other way: the open Galaxy Z Fold 8 and the open iPhone
Duo are wider than they are tall, and their unrotated poses already show the
1020×770 and 890×626 landscape grids - that is what the Portrait column above
holds, since the columns describe the unrotated and rotated poses rather than
the shape of the panel. `orientation="landscape"` stands either upright
(770×1020, 626×890), exactly like turning the real tablet onto its short
edge. Hardware details ride
along: the Tab S11 Ultra's droplet notch, for example, sits on the right edge
in portrait and top center in landscape, matching the physical device.

The phones, foldables, tablets, watches and laptops use a small CSG engine
([`three-bvh-csg`](https://github.com/gkjohnson/three-bvh-csg)) that machines
their USB-C ports, speaker slots, mic holes and band slots into the chassis as
real cavities. It adds ~35 KB gzip to those families and tree-shakes away
entirely when you import only the Studio Display or object mockups, which
have nothing to machine.

## Renderer-agnostic specs

The raw device dimensions are exported as plain data from the `/core` subpath:

```ts
import {
  GALAXY_VARIANTS,
  IPHONE_VARIANTS,
  IPHONE_DUO_VARIANTS,
  FOLD_VARIANTS,
  FLIP_VARIANTS,
  LAPTOP_VARIANTS,
  IPAD_VARIANTS,
  GALAXY_TAB_VARIANTS,
  APPLE_WATCH_VARIANTS,
  GALAXY_WATCH_VARIANTS,
  STUDIO_DISPLAY,
} from 'react-3d-mockups/core'
```

They live on `react-3d-mockups/core`, not the main entry, so importing a spec
never pulls a scene component in with it. The same numbers will drive the
planned 2D (CSS/SVG) renderers.
